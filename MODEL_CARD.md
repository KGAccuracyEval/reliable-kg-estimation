## 2. Intended use

**Primary intended use.** The code estimates the accuracy of a knowledge graph (KG), defined as the proportion of correct triples, from a small annotated sample. It combines simple random sampling, two-stage weighted cluster sampling (TWCS) and stratified TWCS with Wald intervals (baseline) and binomial intervals (Wilson, continuity-corrected Wilson, Agresti-Coull). It supports research on cost-effective and reliable KG quality evaluation.

**Intended users.** Researchers and developers working on data quality and KG evaluation.

**Out-of-scope uses.** This release must not be used for
- clinical diagnosis, prognosis or treatment of individual patients;
- decisions about individuals, such as insurance, employment or access to care;
- re-identification of individuals or linkage with other data sources;
- populations, devices or data types not described in Section 3;
- certifying the correctness of individual triples: the methods estimate aggregate accuracy, not the correctness of single facts.

## 6. Known limitations and risks

- **Generalizability.** Interval reliability was assessed on YAGO, NELL, DisGeNET and SYN 100M. DisGeNET and SYN 100M have no ground truth: their correctness labels are produced by label-generation models (`--generator TEM|CEM`), so results on these KGs depend on those models.
- **Known biases and confounders.** The estimators treat correctness labels as error-free and binary; annotator errors propagate to the estimates. The YAGO sample has accuracy close to 1, the regime in which Wald intervals degenerate.
- **Failure modes.** Wald intervals (baseline) can have zero width or exceed [0, 1] when accuracy is close to 0 or 1. Intervals lose their nominal coverage if the sampling design passed to the code differs from the one used to collect the annotations.
- **Privacy risks.** No personal data and no trained model; membership inference and model inversion tests do not apply.
